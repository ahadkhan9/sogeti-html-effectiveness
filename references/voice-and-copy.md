# Voice and copy

Source: *Sogeti brand guidelines v3*, page 17, plus the live-site voice audit in
[`reference/research/SOGETI_DIGITAL_BRAND_REPORT.md`](../reference/research/SOGETI_DIGITAL_BRAND_REPORT.md).
The rules below are the ones this skill enforces.

---

## 1. The voice in one paragraph

Professional, yet conversational. We talk about complex technology, but we talk
to humans. Read it aloud; if it sounds like a press release, rewrite it. Short,
clear sentences. Brevity and clarity in word choice. A logical flow from one
paragraph to the next. No unnecessary jargon, slang, made-up words or
culture-specific references. International English, American spelling, CMOS as
the reference.

**The Sogeti stance:** hands-on, specific, evidence-led, quietly confident. We
are close enough to the work to know what breaks. We do not oversell, and we do
not hide behind abstraction.

---

## 2. Mechanics

### Sentence case

Capitalize the first word and proper nouns. Do not capitalize for emphasis.
This applies to headlines, buttons, labels, table headers and navigation.

### International English

Use American spelling and punctuation for web and published materials:
*analyze, behavior, capitalize, center, color, favorite, organize, organization,
optimize, program, realize, socialize, theorize, utilize, while, toward.*

### Acronyms

Write out on first mention, then abbreviate:
**Software-as-a-Service (SaaS)**. Exceptions: brand names (IBM) and common
abbreviations (IT).

### Commas

Use the serial comma before the final “and” or “or”:
“our internal clients, alliance partners, and external customers.”

### Quotes and punctuation

Double quotes for spoken word and testimonials. Single quotes for terms or
titles. Commas and periods go inside quotation marks.

### Hyphens and dashes

- Hyphen: joins words (`end-to-end`, `cost-conscious`).
- En dash (–): ranges (`$6–7 million`, `pages 99–101`) and parenthetical
  asides. No spaces around ranges; spaces around a parenthetical dash.
- Em dash (–): **do not use**. It is a strong AI-writing tell and the brand uses
  en dashes. Replace it with a full stop, a comma, a colon or an en dash.

### Slashes

Do not use `/` or `\` to separate items. Write around it with commas, “and” or
“or”. (URLs and file paths are exempt.)

### Currency

Use the symbol before or after the numeral with no space: `$20`, `£30`, `€40`,
`50¢`, `70p`. For larger denominations, do not abbreviate: `US$5 million`,
`€7.5 billion`. Specify non-US dollars (`C$`, `A$`).

### Dates and time

`Month D, YYYY` (November 24, 2023) or `Month D` (April 2). Include the year for
events. Use the 24-hour clock with a time zone: `18:00 CET`. For ranges:
`09:00–14:00 CET | November 24, 2023`.

## 3. Structure

- One idea per paragraph. Two to four sentences is usually enough.
- Lead with the reader's situation, not the company's history.
- Put the most important sentence first.
- Use concrete nouns and active verbs.
- If a sentence needs a second read, cut it.
- If a claim needs a number, provide one. If it needs a source, name it.
- End on the decision or the next action, not on a summary of the summary.

## 4. Banned phrases and replacements

These are not illegal words. They are defaults that make a page sound like every
other AI-assisted page. Use them only with a specific, literal job.

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| elevate your... | improve, shorten, remove, make [specific] |
| seamlessly integrate | integrate without a manual step; name the interface |
| unlock value | reduce [cost] by [amount]; move [metric] |
| empower teams | give [team] the authority to [action] |
| supercharge | accelerate from [x] to [y] |
| game-changing | [specific outcome] in [time] |
| world-class | [evidence: benchmark, client, certification] |
| enterprise-grade | [specific control, scale or SLA] |
| best-in-class | [source and date] |
| cutting-edge | [the actual technology] |
| next-generation | [what replaced what] |
| transform your organization | change [process] for [team] |
| in today's fast-paced world | [the reader's actual situation] |
| it's not just X, it's Y | explain the distinction in a sentence |
| robust, holistic, frictionless, future-proof | say what it does and what it costs |
| leverage (as a verb) | use, build on, apply |
| synergy | shared cost, shared team, shared data |
| data-driven | [which data, which decision] |

**Density is the fingerprint.** One “robust” is not evidence. Three abstractions
in a paragraph plus a rule-of-three plus a spaced em dash is.

## 5. Headline patterns that fit Sogeti

Good Sogeti headlines tend to do one of these:

1. **Name the tension.**
   “The demo is solved. The handover is not.”
2. **Make a specific claim with a mechanism.**
   “One event seam. Two release paths. No big-bang cutover.”
3. **Put the reader in the sentence.**
   “Where does AI delivery actually stand?”
4. **Use a real number with a qualifier.**
   “Nine days to three. Without stopping the book.”
5. **Say the quiet part.**
   “The queue is the product.”
6. **Ask the question the room is avoiding.**
   “Who owns the model on the Monday after go-live?”

Avoid:

- “Unlock the power of AI.”
- “Building the future, together.”
- “Your journey to digital excellence.”
- “Innovate. Transform. Thrive.” (triad)
- “It's not just a platform. It's a partnership.”
- A headline that would survive replacing “Sogeti” with any competitor.

### The logo-swap test

Put a competitor's logo above the headline. If the sentence still works
unchanged, it is not saying anything. Rewrite until it names a specific
situation, mechanism or consequence.

## 6. Sogeti lines, used sparingly

| Line | Use |
|---|---|
| **The magic is in the making** | Cover lines, closing statements, campaign moments |
| **Value in the making** | The idea, not every section heading |
| **Hands-on** | Describe how we work, not as a badge on every page |
| **Making IT work** | Culture and delivery; avoid overuse |
| **Part of the Capgemini Group** | Descriptor; use the approved lockup or sentence |

Do not stack them. One brand line per page is enough. If the page says “the
magic is in the making” and “value in the making” and “hands-on” in the first
two screens, it reads as a slogan generator.

## 7. Proof rules

This is a hard rule for every artifact:

1. **Do not invent a statistic, client, logo, quote, award or certification.**
2. If a number is illustrative, label it visibly: “illustrative scenario”,
   “planning assumption”, “sample data”.
3. Every chart has a source line with an owner and a date, or a clear
   “illustrative” label.
4. Client quotes need written approval and attribution. A composite quote must
   be labelled as a composite.
5. Do not use a competitor's logo or a client logo without permission.
6. Do not imply a Capgemini/Sogeti certification or partnership you cannot
   evidence.
7. If you do not have the fact, write around it. Do not use brackets as a
   substitute for research in a final client artifact; brackets are acceptable
   in a working draft only.

The reference templates use a fictional client, **Northline Group**, and label
it as illustrative. That is the pattern: make the example coherent, and make the
fiction explicit.

## 8. CTA bank

Use a verb and a specific next step. Avoid “Learn more” as the only CTA.

- Book a working session
- Bring us the handover
- Request an invitation
- Read the field note
- Copy the decision record
- Print or save as PDF
- Add to calendar
- Talk to the claims team
- Discuss the proposal
- See the architecture
- Review the options

A CTA should describe what happens next, not manufacture urgency. No “Unlock
now”, “Get started in seconds” or “Don't miss out” unless it is literally true.

## 9. Ten rewrites

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Unlock the power of AI for your business. | Move one AI use case from pilot to a service with an owner. |
| Seamlessly integrate data across the enterprise. | Connect the claims intake to the policy system through one event contract. |
| Our world-class team empowers your journey. | A joint team of six, with the service owner named in week one. |
| Supercharge your claims processing. | Cut intake from nine days to three without pausing the current book. |
| It's not just a platform, it's a partnership. | The platform is the easy part. The operating model is the work. |
| In today's fast-paced world, organizations must transform. | Claims change requests wait six weeks for a release slot. |
| We deliver robust, holistic solutions. | We build the slice, run it for four weeks and hand it to a named owner. |
| Leverage cutting-edge AI to drive efficiency. | Use triage to route exceptions to the right queue, with a tested fallback. |
| A game-changing approach to modernization. | A strangler boundary around the monolith, one slice at a time. |
| Elevate your digital experience. | Reduce the handoffs between intake, assessment and payment. |

## 10. Accessibility of copy

- Link text must make sense out of context. Not “click here”.
- Headings must form a logical outline; do not skip levels for style.
- Do not use colour alone to convey meaning; add a label or icon.
- Expand acronyms on first use.
- Write error messages that say what happened and what to do next.
- Avoid ALL CAPS for long text; screen readers may spell it out.
- Use `lang` attributes for non-English passages.

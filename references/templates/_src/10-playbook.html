Most AI playbooks describe a capability model. This one describes a week. It assumes you already have a use case, a sponsor and a working demo. The job is to turn that into a service that someone owns.

The one rule

Do not scale until one slice has run live for four weeks with a named owner, a tested rollback and one operating metric that moved. Everything else is preparation.

00

Before you start

Four checks that prevent a predictable failure. If any is missing, fix it before writing code.

Field note

The most common failure is a sponsor who cannot say no. If your owner has no authority to stop the service, you have a stakeholder, not an owner.

01

Protect the book

The current service cannot pause for your experiment. Map what must not move before you change anything.

Field note

“No big-bang cutover” is not a strategy on its own. The strategy is a boundary that can be moved one slice at a time.

02

Isolate the slice

Choose the smallest slice that touches a real decision and a real exception. Smaller is better than impressive.

Field note

If the slice needs three new platforms, it is a programme. Cut it until it needs one contract and one owner.

03

Prove it live

Four weeks of real cases beats four months of pre-production testing. Run it with the people who will own it.

Field note

If the operations team cannot run the service without the build team in the room, you have a demo with a support contract.

04

Decide and scale

The decision gate is the point of the 90 days. Make it explicit, documented and reversible.

Field note

A successful gate can still end in “stop.” The value of a 90-day proof is that it makes stopping cheap and evidence-based.

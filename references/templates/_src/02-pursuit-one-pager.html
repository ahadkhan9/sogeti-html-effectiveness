01

Situation

Northline’s claims operation is good at the work it knows. It is also constrained by a platform that treats every change as a full-book event: pricing rules, document handling, payments and correspondence are coupled in the same release cycle.

The result is familiar. The business waits for change, the platform team protects stability, and the best people spend their time on workarounds. Nobody is failing. The architecture is doing what it was designed to do, twenty years after it was designed.

The risk is not the migration. It is the quiet loss of change capacity.

Every quarter spent on a full-platform upgrade is a quarter the claims team cannot respond to a new product, a new regulation or a new channel. The cost is visible in the roadmap, not the run rate.

02

What we heard

In six conversations across claims operations, architecture and finance, four constraints kept returning:

  • The current book cannot pause. Any approach that requires a big-bang cutover is a non-starter for the underwriting and claims leadership team.
  • Batch is a business constraint, not just a technical one. Payment runs and correspondence windows are built around the nightly cycle.
  • Change capacity is the scarce resource. The platform team is fully allocated to regulatory and keep-the-lights work.
  • Nobody wants another programme. The ask is a service with an owner, not a three-year transformation with a steering committee.
03

Approach

We propose a 90-day discovery built around one production slice: first-notice-of-loss intake for a single product line. It is small enough to protect the book and real enough to test the operating model.

01

Protect

Map the current book, freeze the interfaces that cannot move and define the rollback before anything is built.

02

Isolate

Put a bounded slice behind an event contract. Keep the monolith as the system of record until the slice earns its place.

03

Prove

Run the slice live for four weeks with a named operations owner, a fallback and a weekly service review.

04

Scale

Scale by service, not by programme. Each additional slice has its own business case, owner and exit criteria.

05

Why Sogeti

  1. We run what we build

    The same team that designs the slice operates it through the four-week proof. There is no handover to a different delivery group at go-live.

  2. One team, not a relay

    Business analysis, architecture, engineering and operations sit in one squad with one backlog and one weekly decision forum.

  3. Evidence before scale

    Each slice has a baseline, a target and a stop rule. If the evidence is not there, we recommend stopping. That is a feature, not a failure.

  4. Local proximity, global bench

    A local team close enough to sit with claims operations, backed by Capgemini’s engineering and cloud practices when the problem needs them.

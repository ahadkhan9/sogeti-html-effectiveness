# From nine days to three. Without stopping the book. Northline Group, claims intake. A 90-day discovery that became a live service. - **Challenge:** Nine-day intake, 14 manual touches, batch-bound platform. - **Approach:** Strangle one slice, name a service owner, prove live for four weeks. - **Outcomes:** 9→3 days, 41%→70% straight-through, 14→5 touches, €38→€24 per claim. - **Lesson:** The model did not change the queue. The owner did.

01. The challenge

Northline’s claims intake was not broken in the way the dashboard suggested. Claims were not lost. Customers were not ignored. The service was simply slow in a way that had become normal: nine days from first notice to a complete file, fourteen handoffs and a nightly batch that set the rhythm for everyone.

The initial ask was familiar: “Can AI triage the claims?” The honest answer was yes, but triage would not fix a nine-day queue on its own. The constraint was the handoff between intake, assessment and payment. A model could classify a document; it could not own the exception.

The brief changed in week one

We reframed the engagement from “build a triage model” to “move one intake slice, with its owner and its queue.” The model became one component of the service, not the point of it.

02. The approach

We protected the current book, isolated first-notice-of-loss intake for one product line, and put it behind an event contract. The monolith stayed the system of record. The slice ran live for four weeks with a named operations owner, a tested rollback and a weekly service review.

The team was deliberately small: a solution lead, two engineers, a business analyst, an operations owner and a part-time risk partner. The steering group met three times. Most decisions were made in the daily stand-up, not in a committee.

03. The outcomes

At the week 12 gate, the first slice had processed live claims for four weeks. The numbers below are client-reported and cover that slice only. They are not a forecast for the whole claims function.

The model did not change the queue. The owner did. Once someone owned the exception, the technology had somewhere to land.

04. What we would do differently

  • Fund the exception queue on day one. We waited until week 5 to add analyst capacity. The queue was the binding constraint from the first live case.
  • Bring risk into the rollback rehearsal earlier. The second rehearsal convinced risk. The first would have saved two weeks of questions.
  • Retire the old path sooner. The dual-run period lasted three weeks longer than planned. Keeping both paths alive is a quiet tax on the team.

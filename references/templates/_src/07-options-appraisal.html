The model is not the decision

A weighted matrix makes assumptions explicit. It does not validate them. Before the decision, test the two highest-weighted criteria against evidence: a reference client, a costed plan or a live proof. If you cannot, the score is a preference.

02 / What the model does not capture

Three things a score cannot tell you.

The matrix is useful because it is narrow. These are the questions it deliberately leaves outside.

01

Reversibility

A slightly lower score with a clean exit is often better than a higher score that locks you in. Ask what it costs to stop.

02

Organizational fit

The best option on paper can fail on a governance model that cannot staff it. Weight the operating model, not just the build.

03

The cost of waiting

Every option has a clock. A decision delayed by a quarter is a fourth option with its own cost and risk.

Northline Group is an illustrative client scenario. Scores and weights are a teaching example, not a recommendation. Replace them with evidence before a real decision.
